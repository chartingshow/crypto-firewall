# 🚨 Crypto Investment Cash Courier Scam

Cryptocurrency investment scams are increasingly combining **online investment fraud with physical cash collection**.

In this type of scam, criminals first convince the victim that they are making legitimate cryptocurrency investments. After gaining the victim's trust, the scammers instruct them to withdraw large amounts of cash and hand the money to a **courier sent to their home or another meeting location**.

The courier may even use a **password, code or banknote serial number** to make the collection appear legitimate.

> ⚠️ **Never hand cash, cryptocurrency, precious metals or other valuables to a courier as part of an investment.**

## 🧠 How the Scam Works

### 1. The Scammer Makes Contact

The scam often begins through:

- Social media
- Dating platforms
- Professional networking websites
- Messaging applications
- Unsolicited text messages
- "Wrong number" messages
- Fake cryptocurrency investment experts
- Fake business or investment opportunities

The scammer may pretend to be looking for friendship, romance or a business relationship.

Their objective is to establish communication and gradually gain the victim's trust.

### 2. The Scammer Builds Trust

The scammer may communicate with the victim for days, weeks or even months before asking for money.

Common techniques include:

- Frequent friendly conversations
- Romantic attention
- Claims of shared interests
- Displays of wealth or financial success
- Cryptocurrency trading screenshots
- Claims of successful investments
- Promises to teach the victim how to invest

Some scammers use **love bombing**, where excessive praise and attention are used to quickly create emotional trust.

### 3. A Cryptocurrency Investment Is Introduced

Once trust has been established, the scammer introduces cryptocurrency investing.

The victim may be instructed to:

- Download a particular trading application
- Create an account on a cryptocurrency website
- Open an account with a supposed investment company
- Transfer money to bank accounts controlled by the scammers
- Purchase cryptocurrency
- Follow trading instructions supplied by the scammer

The investment website or application may look extremely convincing.

## 📈 Fake Profits Appear

The fraudulent platform may show the victim that their investment is rapidly increasing in value.

For example:

```text
Initial investment:     $5,000
Displayed balance:     $12,500
Displayed profit:       $7,500
```

These figures may be completely fictitious.

The scammers control the website or investment system and can display whatever balance they want.

Seeing apparent profits encourages the victim to deposit larger amounts.

> ⚠️ A balance displayed on an investment website does not prove that the money or cryptocurrency actually exists.

## 🏦 The Bank May Stop the Transfer

Banks and financial institutions may identify suspicious transactions and refuse or delay further transfers.

Instead of abandoning the scam, the criminals may provide another way to send the money.

The victim may be told:

- Their investment account has been "flagged"
- Bank transfers are temporarily unavailable
- Additional verification is required
- A fine must be paid
- Taxes must be paid
- A withdrawal fee must be paid
- A security deposit is required
- Cash must be deposited manually

The scammer then instructs the victim to withdraw physical cash.

## 🚗 A Courier Is Sent to Collect the Cash

Once the victim confirms that the cash is ready, the scammer arranges for someone to collect it.

The courier may meet the victim:

- At their home
- Outside their home
- At a public location
- In a car park
- Near a bank or other agreed meeting point

The courier may know very little about the wider scam and may simply have been instructed where to collect the money.

## 🔑 Courier Authentication Codes

To make the transaction appear secure and legitimate, scammers may give the victim an authentication method.

For example:

```text
Password: ORANGE-4721
```

or they may provide the serial number from a banknote.

The courier then provides the matching password or displays the matching banknote when arriving.

This can make the victim believe that the courier is genuinely connected to the investment company.

> 🚨 A matching password, code or banknote serial number does **not** make the transaction legitimate.

The code was created by the scammers themselves.

## 🔄 The Fake Investment Balance Increases Again

After the courier takes the cash, the victim may see their online investment balance increase.

This creates the illusion that the cash was successfully deposited into the investment.

The victim may therefore believe that the courier transaction was legitimate.

However, the numbers displayed on the fraudulent platform may simply be changed by the scammers.

## 💸 The Withdrawal Trap

The fraud often becomes obvious when the victim attempts to withdraw their supposed investment profits.

The victim may suddenly be told that they must pay:

- Taxes
- Withdrawal charges
- Processing fees
- Account verification fees
- Penalties
- Security deposits
- Anti-money-laundering fees
- Additional investment requirements

Another courier may then be sent to collect more cash.

This cycle can continue repeatedly until the victim refuses to pay or runs out of accessible money.

> ❌ **Do not pay additional money to unlock or withdraw funds from a suspected fraudulent investment platform.**

Paying another fee usually results in another demand.

# 🚩 Major Warning Signs

Treat an investment as potentially fraudulent if someone:

- Contacts you unexpectedly and later introduces cryptocurrency investing
- Claims unusually high or consistent investment returns
- Directs you to a specific unknown cryptocurrency website or application
- Controls or guides every step of your investment
- Tells you to transfer money to unrelated bank accounts
- Claims your investment account has suddenly been "flagged"
- Requests taxes or fees before allowing withdrawals
- Tells you to withdraw large amounts of cash
- Sends someone to physically collect money
- Uses passwords or banknote serial numbers to identify a courier
- Tells you not to discuss the investment with your bank, family or authorities
- Continually asks for additional payments

Multiple warning signs substantially increase the likelihood that the investment is fraudulent.

# 🛡️ Protect Yourself

Never give an unknown person:

- Your home address
- Banking information
- Cryptocurrency wallet credentials
- Private keys
- Seed or recovery phrases
- Identification documents unless you have independently verified who is requesting them

Do not continue conversations that began through unsolicited or suspicious communications simply because the person appears friendly or knowledgeable.

Independently investigate cryptocurrency investment platforms before sending money.

Search for:

```text
Platform name + scam
Platform name + fraud
Website address + scam
Website address + review
Company name + warning
```

Do not rely solely on reviews displayed on the investment company's own website.

# 🚨 If a Courier Is Already Coming

If you have been instructed to withdraw cash and a courier is already scheduled to collect it:

**Do not hand over the money.**

Stop communicating with the scammer and do not tell them where you are going.

Do not invite the courier into your home.

If you believe someone is travelling to your home to collect money as part of a fraud, contact your local police or appropriate law-enforcement authority.

Do not confront or attempt to detain the courier yourself.

Keep any messages containing:

- Meeting locations
- Collection times
- Courier descriptions
- Vehicle information
- Telephone numbers
- Passwords
- Authentication codes
- Banknote serial numbers

These may be useful evidence.

# 🆘 If You Already Handed Over Cash

Act quickly.

Contact your:

1. Bank or financial institution
2. Cryptocurrency exchange, if cryptocurrency was involved
3. Local police or fraud-reporting authority

Explain that you believe you are the victim of an investment scam involving an in-person cash courier.

Do **not** send additional money to recover the original payment.

Be particularly cautious of anyone who subsequently contacts you claiming they can recover your stolen cryptocurrency or money for an upfront fee.

Victims of cryptocurrency investment fraud are frequently targeted again by **recovery scammers**.

# 🗂️ Preserve Evidence

Do not delete your conversations with the scammers.

Save as much information as possible, including:

```text
Names and usernames
Social-media profiles
Telephone numbers
Email addresses
Messaging-app accounts
Website addresses
Cryptocurrency platform names
Cryptocurrency wallet addresses
Transaction hashes
Bank account details
Recipient names
Payment instructions
Courier meeting locations
Courier descriptions
Vehicle details
Passwords or authentication codes
Screenshots
Receipts
Bank-transfer records
Cryptocurrency transaction records
```

Screenshots of the fraudulent investment platform can also be valuable, particularly pages showing balances, transactions, withdrawal demands or payment instructions.

# 🇺🇸 Reporting in the United States

Victims in the United States should report cryptocurrency investment fraud to the **FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3)**.

Provide as much information as possible about:

- The person or company that contacted you
- Websites and applications used
- Email addresses
- Telephone numbers
- Social-media or messaging profiles
- Bank accounts used to receive money
- Cryptocurrency wallet addresses
- Recipient names
- Screenshots and messages

Victims aged **60 or over** who require assistance filing an IC3 complaint can also contact the **U.S. Department of Justice Elder Justice Hotline**:

```text
1-833-FRAUD-11
1-833-372-8311
```

# 🌍 Reporting Outside the United States

If you are outside the United States, report the fraud to your country's:

- National fraud-reporting service
- Cybercrime authority
- Financial regulator
- Local police

You should also immediately contact any bank or cryptocurrency exchange involved in the transactions.

# 🧱 Crypto Firewall Limitations

Crypto Firewall can help block known:

- Scam websites
- Phishing domains
- Fraudulent cryptocurrency infrastructure
- Malicious URLs

However, a blocklist cannot completely protect against this type of attack.

Cash-courier investment scams rely heavily on **social engineering**, telephone calls, messaging applications and physical interactions.

Attackers can also continuously create new websites, accounts and infrastructure.

Crypto Firewall should therefore be used as part of a **layered security strategy** rather than as the only protection against cryptocurrency fraud.

# ✅ Remember

**A legitimate cryptocurrency investment should not require you to hand bundles of cash to a stranger sent to your home.**

If an investment company suddenly requires physical cash collection, stop and independently verify everything before taking any further action.

Never allow urgency, apparent profits, romantic pressure or supposed account penalties to prevent you from independently checking where your money is actually going.
